Welcome!

Our lab investigates the cognitive and neural mechanisms underlying language processing across the lifespan, with the goal of developing a comprehensive understanding of how language is represented and processed in the brain. We are particularly interested in how language-specific properties, individual differences, and aging shape the relationship between language and the brain. To address these questions, we adopt a multimodal approach that integrates neuroimaging techniques, such as EEG and fMRI, with behavioral measures to study language processing across diverse populations.

Research Interests

Neural Representation of Language

Examining how language is represented in the brain and how language-specific properties influence its neural organization and processing.

Individual Differences in Language Processing

Exploring how differences in cognitive abilities, linguistic experience, language history, and other individual characteristics influence language comprehension and its neural correlates.

Aging and Language Comprehension

Examining how healthy aging affects language comprehension and the neural mechanisms supporting language processing, with a particular focus on why individuals differ in age-related changes in language abilities.
