Current Members

Principal Investigator

Mohammad Momenian

I have two main research strands as a neurolinguist. One is to look into how language-specific properties have an effect on the neural representation of languages in the brain. The other, more recent, strand focuses on the role of individual differences in modifying age-related decline in language comprehension in the older population. I use different neuroimaging modalities such as EEG and fMRI along with ecologically valid behavioural measurements. Using a multimodal approach, I endeavour to present a thorough picture of the language and brain across the lifespan amid diverse populations.

Email: momenian@hku.hk

Ph.D. Students

Qu Xingfang

My research interests focus on the cognitive and neural mechanisms underlying language processing. I currently use simultaneous EEG-fMRI to investigate the spatiotemporal dynamics of language comprehension in older adults. I am also interested in the neural mechanisms of syntactic processing and the use of neuromodulation techniques, such as TMS and tDCS, to investigate language processing.

Email: u3014342@connect.hku.hk

Yike WANG

My research is at the intersection of artificial intelligence and cognitive neuroscience, where I use Large Language Models (LLMs) to investigate the neural basis of human language processing. By integrating computational modeling with neuroimaging data (e.g., fMRI and EEG/MEG), I examine how the brain represents and processes language during naturalistic comprehension.

Email: yike.wang@connect.hku.hk

Edgar

I am a part-time Student Assistant at the lab and a Cantonese-speaking speech therapy student based in Hong Kong. With academic training in speech and language therapy, I support research on language processing across different age groups through data processing, experimental stimulus preparation, participant recruitment and scheduling, as well as organizing data and materials for analysis.

A key part of my role is creating a positive and supportive experience for participants and their families by providing clear and timely communication, answering questions about study procedures, and helping them feel comfortable throughout their participation. My goal is to contribute to rigorous and meaningful research that advances our understanding of communication development while ensuring that research participation remains accessible, respectful, and engaging.
